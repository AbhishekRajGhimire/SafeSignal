# SYNCS HACK 2026 — CHALLENGE BRIEF

## 01. Challenge

### Challenge Theme

**Blocks**

The challenge invites teams to explore how technology can remove barriers, connect people, improve access, preserve culture, and help communities make better use of resources.

The goal is **not simply to build an application that technically answers one question**.

The goal is to identify a meaningful real-world problem within the challenge space and build a technology solution that is:

* Useful
* Human-centred
* Accessible
* Technically strong
* Innovative
* Visually polished
* Reliable
* Demonstrable
* Memorable

---

# 02. Official Exploration Blocks

The following are the official challenge directions provided for the hackathon.

### Block 1 — Sharing Underused Resources

> What technology solutions help communities share underused resources, skills, knowledge, or infrastructure?

Potential areas of exploration:

* Community resource sharing
* Equipment sharing
* Skill exchange
* Knowledge exchange
* Community spaces
* Local infrastructure
* Volunteer networks
* Peer-to-peer assistance
* Time and expertise sharing
* Reducing waste through better utilisation

Key question:

**What valuable resources already exist but are currently inaccessible, disconnected, or underused?**

---

### Block 2 — Connecting With Our Past and Other Cultures

> How can we use technology to bring us closer to our past and connect with other cultures?

Potential areas of exploration:

* Cultural heritage
* Local history
* Family stories
* Oral histories
* Indigenous knowledge
* Languages
* Traditions
* Migration stories
* Cultural artefacts
* Historical places
* Intergenerational knowledge
* Cross-cultural learning

Key question:

**How can technology preserve, discover, experience, and share knowledge that might otherwise be lost or disconnected from younger generations?**

---

### Block 3 — Genuine Human Connection

> How, in a world where technology begins to isolate us, can we find genuine connection with each other?

Potential areas of exploration:

* Community connection
* Loneliness
* Social isolation
* Local communities
* Intergenerational connection
* Shared experiences
* Volunteering
* Peer support
* Real-world activities
* Meaningful conversations
* Community participation

Key question:

**Can technology be designed to move people toward genuine human interaction rather than keeping them inside a screen?**

---

### Block 4 — Accessibility to Essential Resources

> What tools can we use to ensure accessibility to our essential resources?

Potential areas of exploration:

* Essential services
* Education
* Food
* Transport
* Housing
* Community services
* Healthcare navigation
* Public information
* Emergency resources
* Digital services
* Local support

Key question:

**What prevents people from accessing something they fundamentally need, and how can technology reduce that barrier?**

---

### Block 5 — Accessible Information and Digital Services

> How can we make information and digital services more accessible to people with different abilities, languages, ages, or levels of digital literacy?

Potential areas of exploration:

* Accessibility
* Disability
* Language barriers
* Translation
* Digital literacy
* Elderly users
* Children
* Neurodiverse users
* Low-literacy users
* Migrants
* Multilingual communities
* Simplified interfaces
* Voice interfaces
* Visual interfaces
* Assistive technology

Key question:

**How can the same information or service become understandable and usable for people with fundamentally different needs and abilities?**

# 03. Core Challenge Interpretation

Claude must interpret **Blocks** as a human-centred technology challenge.

The strongest solutions should not merely demonstrate technology.

They should demonstrate:

**Technology → removes a barrier → creates access/connection → produces meaningful human impact.**

Avoid building technology for technology's sake.

---

# 04. Strategic Opportunity

A particularly strong project may connect multiple Blocks simultaneously.

For example:

**Accessibility + Community + Cultural Knowledge**

or:

**Underused Resources + Human Connection + Accessibility**

or:

**Culture + Language + Digital Accessibility**

or:

**Resources + Digital Literacy + Community**

Claude should actively look for these intersections during ideation.

---

# 05. Cross-Block Opportunity Matrix

When generating ideas, evaluate whether an idea can naturally address multiple Blocks.

| Block               | Primary Opportunity                                            |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sharing resources   | Unlock what communities already have                           |
| Culture & history   | Preserve and reconnect knowledge                               |
| Human connection    | Turn digital interaction into meaningful real-world connection |
| Essential resources | Reduce barriers to critical services                           |
| Accessibility       | Make information usable by more people                         |

A strong concept may have:

* One primary Block
* One or two secondary Blocks

Do **not** force an idea to cover every Block.

Depth is more important than artificial breadth.

# 06. What Makes a Strong Concept?

Claude should favour ideas that demonstrate the following characteristics.

### 1. Real Problem

The problem should affect real people.

Avoid hypothetical problems created only to justify a technology.

---

### 2. Clear User

The project should have a clearly defined primary user.

Avoid:

> "Everyone."

Prefer:

> "Older residents who struggle to access local digital services."

or:

> "New migrants who need to navigate essential community resources."

---

### 3. Strong Human Outcome

The product should create a meaningful outcome.

Examples:

* Someone gains access to something they previously couldn't use.
* Someone discovers knowledge from their community.
* Someone connects with another person.
* A previously unused resource gets utilised.
* A cultural story is preserved.
* A person with a disability can independently access information.

---

### 4. Technology With Purpose

Technology should solve something that would otherwise be difficult.

Potential technologies may include:

* AI
* Computer vision
* Natural language processing
* Speech recognition
* Translation
* Recommendation systems
* Geospatial technology
* Data visualisation
* Accessibility technology
* APIs
* Automation
* Multimodal interfaces

However:

**Technology choice must follow the problem.**

Do not select a technology first and invent a problem around it.

---

### 5. Demonstrable

The concept must be possible to demonstrate clearly during the hackathon.

A judge should be able to understand:

**Problem → Action → Technology → Result → Impact**

within a short demonstration.

---

# 07. Innovation Principles

Innovation does not necessarily mean inventing a completely new technology.

Innovation may come from:

* A new combination of existing technologies
* A new interaction model
* Removing an existing barrier
* Making an existing service dramatically easier to use
* Connecting previously disconnected communities
* Turning passive information into an active experience
* Using AI to personalise accessibility
* Converting digital interaction into real-world connection
* Giving underused resources a new distribution mechanism

Claude should distinguish between:

### Technology novelty

"What technology are we using?"

and:

### Problem-solving novelty

"Why does this solution approach the problem differently?"

The second is more important.

---

# 08. Accessibility Must Be Considered From the Beginning

Accessibility should not be added at the end.

For every concept, Claude should ask:

* Can someone with limited vision use it?
* Can someone with limited hearing use it?
* Can someone with limited mobility use it?
* Can someone with limited digital literacy understand it?
* Can someone who speaks another language use it?
* Can an older person navigate it?
* Can someone using a keyboard operate it?
* Can someone using assistive technology understand it?
* Can the interface work under reduced-motion preferences?
* Is important information available in more than one format?

Accessibility should be treated as a **core product capability**, not a compliance checklist.

---

# 09. Human Connection Principle

Because one of the challenge blocks specifically addresses technology-driven isolation, Claude should ask:

> **Does our technology bring people closer together, or does it simply give people another screen to stare at?**

Prefer solutions that:

* Encourage real-world interaction
* Facilitate meaningful conversations
* Connect people with shared interests
* Encourage community participation
* Support intergenerational exchange
* Help people discover local opportunities
* Turn digital discovery into physical participation

If a product increases screen time without creating meaningful value, reconsider the concept.

# 10. Cultural Responsibility

For ideas involving culture, history, Indigenous knowledge, community stories, or personal histories:

Claude must consider:

* Consent
* Ownership
* Attribution
* Context
* Cultural sensitivity
* Privacy
* Misrepresentation
* Appropriate sourcing

Never fabricate cultural information.

Never present generated information as historical fact without verification.

Never treat culture as merely a visual aesthetic.

For resource-sharing ideas, consider:

### What is underused?

Examples:

* Equipment
* Knowledge
* Skills
* Spaces
* Time
* Transport
* Community facilities
* Digital resources

### Why is it underused?

Potential barriers:

* People don't know it exists
* Poor discoverability
* Trust
* Location
* Scheduling
* Accessibility
* Cost
* Language
* Digital literacy
* Coordination

The product should address the actual barrier rather than simply creating another marketplace.

# 13. Digital Accessibility Principles

For accessibility-focused concepts, avoid assuming that:

> "Accessibility = larger text."

Accessibility may involve:

* Language
* Cognitive load
* Navigation
* Voice
* Visual presentation
* Audio
* Reading level
* Translation
* Interaction complexity
* Digital confidence
* Motor accessibility
* Hearing accessibility
* Vision accessibility

The strongest solutions may provide the **same information through multiple accessible interaction modes**.

Score each idea from 1–10.

| Criterion               | Weight |

| Human impact            |    15% |
| Innovation              |    15% |
| Technical depth         |    10% |
| Feasibility             |    15% |
| Demo potential          |    10% |
| Accessibility potential |     5% |
| Visual/WOW potential    |     5% |

# 16. Red Flags

Claude should challenge ideas that:

* Are generic
* Already have obvious existing solutions
* Require enormous infrastructure
* Depend on unavailable data
* Require too many integrations
* Have unclear users
* Have weak human impact
* Are difficult to demonstrate
* Depend entirely on unreliable APIs
* Use AI without a meaningful reason
* Add unnecessary complexity
* Ignore accessibility
* Are essentially a CRUD application with a different theme
* Are visually impressive but solve little
* Solve a problem that doesn't actually exist

# 17. Scope Rule

The team has limited hackathon time.

Therefore:

### P0 — Must Have

Without this, the product does not work.

### P1 — High Value

Strongly improves the product or judging outcome.

### P2 — Polish

Useful improvements if time permits.

### CUT

Remove if time becomes constrained.

The team should **reduce scope before reducing quality**.

# 20. Design Requirements

All interfaces created for the project must follow the project's master design system.

Core philosophy:

> "I used to believe typography was about making text look good. It's not. It's about making information clear, structured, and effortless to read."

Design requirements include:

* Clear hierarchy
* Intentional typography
* Maximum 2–3 font families
* Limited font weights
* 1.4 type-scale reference
* Intentional tracking
* Comfortable line-height
* 3×3 composition framework
* Meaningful whitespace
* 60–30–10 colour balance
* Strong contrast
* Responsive layouts
* Accessibility
* Light-mode testing
* Dark-mode testing
* Low-brightness testing
* Small-screen testing

Use the detailed design instructions in:

`.claude/skills/design-system/`

---

# 21. Animation Requirements

When animation provides meaningful value, use **GSAP**.

Animation should communicate:

* Hierarchy
* State
* Feedback
* Navigation
* Transformation
* Storytelling

Preferred techniques:

* GSAP timelines
* Staggered reveals
* ScrollTrigger
* Transform animation
* Opacity
* Scale
* Translate

Avoid unnecessary animation.

Aim for **1–2 memorable WOW moments**, rather than animating every element.

Always respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

# 22. Technology Selection

The technology stack must be selected after the problem and product concept are understood.

Evaluate technology according to:

1. Speed of implementation
2. Team familiarity
3. Reliability
4. Demo stability
5. Integration requirements
6. Deployment simplicity
7. Performance
8. Security
9. Accessibility
10. Future extensibility

Do not choose a technology because it is fashionable.
